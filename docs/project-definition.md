# CivicFlow — Project Definition

## 1. Project Overview

**Product name:** CivicFlow

**Product type:** Independent digital service discovery platform

**Project stage:** Discovery and UX research

**Purpose:** Explore how people can find relevant local services and understand the steps required to access them through a clear, accessible and guided digital experience.

CivicFlow is a fictional portfolio concept. It is not an official government service.

## 2. Problem Statement

People may struggle to identify the right local service when information is spread across multiple websites, eligibility requirements are unclear, or information is presented using unfamiliar terminology.

This can create uncertainty about where to start, what information is required and what to do next.

We need to investigate these problems before deciding which features to build.

## 3. Design Challenge

How might we help people identify relevant local services, understand their options and navigate the next steps with confidence, while ensuring the experience is accessible and inclusive?

## 4. Initial Target Users

### User Group A — People unfamiliar with service terminology

People who understand their problem but do not know the name of the service or organisation that can help them.

### User Group B — People who need a straightforward digital journey

People who want to find information, understand requirements and complete a task without unnecessary steps.

### User Group C — People with different access needs

People who may use keyboard navigation, screen readers, browser zoom, mobile devices or other accessibility features.

These are initial assumptions about potential users, not validated research findings.

## 5. Initial Hypotheses

- People may find goal-based questions easier than navigating organisational categories.
- Explaining requirements before a form begins may help people prepare.
- Clear progress indicators may help people understand where they are in a journey.
- Accessible language, predictable interactions and useful error messages may reduce confusion.

Each hypothesis must be investigated through research or usability testing.

## 6. Proposed Solution

Explore a guided service discovery experience that allows users to:

1. Describe what they need help with.
2. Explore relevant service options.
3. Understand requirements and possible eligibility.
4. See what information they need before starting.
5. Follow a clear, accessible digital journey.
6. Understand the next steps after completing a task.

The final feature set will depend on research findings.

## 7. Success Measures

We will evaluate the prototype using task-based usability testing.

Potential measures include:

- Task completion rate.
- Time taken to complete a task.
- Number and type of errors.
- Requests for assistance.
- User understanding of the next step.
- Accessibility issues identified during testing.

We will establish a baseline before setting improvement targets. We will report actual findings rather than inventing results.

## 8. Constraints and Principles

- Use plain, understandable language.
- Design for mobile and desktop.
- Consider accessibility from the beginning.
- Protect participant privacy during research.
- Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.
- Do not imply that CivicFlow provides official eligibility decisions.
- Validate assumptions before treating them as facts.

## 9. Initial Deliverables

- Research plan and interview questions.
- Research notes and evidence matrix.
- User journey and service blueprint.
- Information architecture and user flows.
- Low- and high-fidelity wireframes.
- Interactive prototype.
- Accessibility review.
- Usability test findings and design iterations.
- Working responsive web application.
- Public portfolio case study.

## 10. Open Questions

- Which service category should the first release focus on?
- How do users currently find relevant services?
- Which information causes the most confusion?
- What should the prototype help users accomplish first?
- Which accessibility needs should influence the initial design?

These questions will guide the next research step.
