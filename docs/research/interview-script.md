# CivicFlow — User Interview Script

## Introduction

Thank you for taking part. I'm exploring how people find local services online and understand what steps they need to take.

I'm interested in your experiences, not in testing you. There are no right or wrong answers. You can skip any question or stop at any time.

I'll take anonymised notes for my portfolio project. Please don't share private information about yourself or anyone else. I won't record audio or video unless we discuss it and you explicitly agree.

Do you understand the purpose of this session, and are you happy to participate and have anonymised notes used in my portfolio?

## Section A — Recent experience

1. Can you tell me about the last time you searched online for a local service or support?
2. What were you trying to achieve?
3. Where did you start looking?
4. What did you do next?

## Section B — Difficulties and decisions

5. Was anything difficult or confusing?
6. How did you decide which information or website to trust?
7. Was there anything you expected to find but couldn't?
8. What did you do when you weren't sure what to do next?

## Section C — Information and confidence

9. What information did you need before you could take the next step?
10. How did you find out whether the service was relevant to your situation?
11. Did you need to prepare any information or documents?
12. What helped you feel confident enough to continue?

## Section D — Access and devices

13. Which device did you use?
14. Were there any difficulties reading, navigating or completing the process?
15. Did you use any browser settings or assistive tools that helped you?

Only ask about accessibility in a way that feels comfortable to the participant. They do not need to disclose a diagnosis or disability.

## Closing questions

16. What was the most difficult part of the experience?
17. What, if anything, would have made the process easier?
18. Is there anything else about finding local services online that I should understand?

## Interviewer reminders

- Ask about real past experiences rather than hypothetical preferences.
- Use neutral follow-up questions.
- Avoid suggesting answers.
- Allow participants time to think.
- Separate observations, direct quotes and interpretations in the notes.
- Thank the participant for their time.
