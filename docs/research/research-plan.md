# CivicFlow — User Research Plan

## 1. Research objective

Understand how people discover local support services, evaluate which service is relevant and identify the next steps they need to take.

## 2. Research questions

1. How do people currently find local services?
2. What information do they look for first?
3. What makes it difficult to identify the right service?
4. Which words or phrases are confusing?
5. What information do people need before starting an application?
6. What helps people feel confident about their next step?
7. What accessibility barriers might prevent task completion?

## 3. Research method

Conduct short, semi-structured interviews followed by a task-based usability evaluation of an early CivicFlow prototype when one is available.

For the initial discovery phase, focus on interviews and recent experiences. Do not present opinions about an imagined interface as evidence of actual usability.

## 4. Participants

Target: 3–5 participants for exploratory research.

Recruit adults with varied experiences of finding online services.

Participation is voluntary. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal or sensitive information.

## 5. Interview duration

Approximately 15–20 minutes per participant.

## 6. Interview questions

- Think about the last time you searched online for a local service. What were you trying to do?
- Where did you start looking?
- How did you decide which website or service to trust?
- Was anything confusing or difficult?
- What information did you need before taking the next step?
- Did you know whether you could access the service?
- What did you do when you could not find an answer?
- Did you complete the task? Why or why not?
- What would have made the experience easier?
- Is there anything else you think a service website should do to help people?

Use neutral follow-up questions such as “Can you tell me more?” and “What happened next?”

## 7. Consent and privacy

Before beginning, explain:

- The purpose of the research.
- That participation is voluntary.
- That participants can skip questions or stop at any time.
- What notes will be recorded and how they will be used.
- Whether any recording is proposed and obtain explicit permission before recording.

Use participant codes rather than names in research notes. Do not collect real application details, addresses, financial information or other unnecessary sensitive data.

## 8. Research analysis

After each interview:

1. Record observations as accurately as possible.
2. Separate direct observations from participant opinions and researcher interpretations.
3. Identify recurring problems and differences between participants.
4. Group related observations into themes.
5. Link each proposed design decision to supporting evidence.
6. Record uncertainties and questions requiring further research.

## 9. Deliverables

- Anonymised research notes.
- An evidence matrix.
- Prioritised user needs.
- Validated and unvalidated assumptions.
- Design opportunities.
- A list of questions for further investigation.

## 10. Limitations

A small exploratory sample can reveal useful usability issues and generate hypotheses, but it cannot establish that findings apply to everyone.

Do not report success rates, time savings or other quantitative improvements until they have been measured.
